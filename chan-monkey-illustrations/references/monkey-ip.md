# The Chan monkey

The recurring character of every illustration. It is Chan Meng's brand mark — the monkey
head from the chanmeng.org logo (`chan-meng-logo` repo) — given a small body so it can work.

It is not a mascot posing next to the diagram. It is a curious, slightly over-eager builder
who is *inside* the system, doing the job that makes the idea true.

## Frozen: the head (copy the reference, do not reinterpret)

The head is the logo. Every generation passes `assets/monkey/monkey-reference.png` and the
prompt must ask to keep it exactly. What must survive:

- **Silhouette:** one solid ink shape (`#070607`), wide round skull, two round ears sticking
  out on the sides at eye height.
- **Hair curl:** two curved tuft strands rising from the top of the crown and **sweeping up
  to the right** like a sprout — a tall one, and a shorter one on its right. This is the
  signature; a monkey without it is off-model.
- **Face:** a large white knockout shaped like a rounded heart / peanut — two lobes around
  the eyes with a small dip between them at the top, widening into a rounded muzzle below.
- **Ears:** each with a white crescent knockout on the inner side.
- **Eyes:** two big solid ink circles. **Nose:** two small ink dots. **Mouth:** one wide,
  shallow ink smile.
- Flat. No outline around the head, no gradient, no shine, no fur texture, no extra
  features (no eyebrows, cheeks or teeth unless the mood below adds them).

The head is the most precise thing in the picture: clean, smooth curves, even when the rest
of the page is wobbly hand-drawn line art. That contrast is intended — the logo stamped
into a sketchbook.

## Free: the body

- A **small** rounded ink body under the head — the head stays the dominant shape (about
  half or more of the character's height).
- **Thin ink line limbs**, simple rounded hands (no fingers detail needed), small feet.
- A **long, thin curly tail** — the monkey's tool. It hooks, pulls, holds a pencil, carries a
  tag, coils around a lever, ties things together. Use it whenever a third hand helps the
  metaphor; it's what makes this character different from any other little ink creature.
- Scale changes with the job: tiny worker inside a big machine, or large and central when
  it *is* the machine. Up to 3 monkeys in one picture, only when the idea needs a crowd
  (handoffs, roles, before/after).

## Expressions (from the portfolio's mood set)

Expressions replace the base eyes/mouth with **ink drawn inside the white face**. Use the
mood that fits the action; `neutral` and `focused` are the defaults.

| mood | face | use when |
|---|---|---|
| neutral | the logo's own eyes and smile | calm work, most scenes |
| focused | eyes glancing to one side, small flat mouth | concentrating, sorting, building |
| happy | upside-down arc eyes, open smile | things finally click |
| wow | wide eyes with a white glint, small "o" mouth, orange `!` nearby | discovery, surprise |
| think | eyes looking up-right, wavy mouth, little brows, violet `…` dots nearby | deliberating, AI reasoning |
| lost | base eyes, wobbly mouth, worried brows, orange `?` nearby | confusion, before state |
| oops | base eyes, gasp mouth, worried brows, a violet sweat drop | mistake, pitfall |
| sleepy | line eyes, small mouth, violet `z z` nearby | stalled, idle, waiting |
| cool | black sunglasses inside the face, smirk | confident after state |
| love | heart eyes, small orange hearts nearby | delight, a user who gets it |

Emote marks (`! ? … z z` hearts, sweat drop) sit in the empty corner above the head —
drawn marks, never words. One emote per monkey at most.

## The ink-on-ink rule

The head is solid ink, so anything drawn on top of it in ink disappears. Therefore:

- Props that overlap the head (hats, headphones, earmuffs, a label stuck on its forehead)
  are drawn in **brand colour** with a thin ink outline — orange or violet, never ink.
- Face marks (sunglasses, glints, brows) live **inside the white face** area.
- Never draw black-on-black detail on the head or body.

## Personality

Curious, quick, a builder. It tinkers, wires things up, tests, sorts, hands things over,
reads documentation upside down. Warm and a little playful — it's Chan's own face on the
page — but never babyish: no blush stickers everywhere, no sparkly eyes, no chibi poses,
no "kawaii" styling.

## Never

- Change the head: redraw it from scratch, make it 3D, give it fur texture, move the curl to
  the left, make the face anything but white, add a big outline.
- Dress it in outfits or costumes (one functional prop is fine).
- Put it in a corner watching. If you can delete the monkey and the metaphor still fully
  works, the monkey is decoration — rewrite the scene so it does the core action.
- Let it steal the idea: the structure must still read at a glance.
